## The line

> I am dying mid-call with your mother. She thinks you hung up on her. That is your problem now.

Why this one, after drafting 33 candidates and judging them pairwise against the brief (see `candidates.md`):

- **It is the phone talking.** First person, mid-call, about its own dying battery — not a narrator, not a caption.
- **It turns twice.** First the scene: dying in the middle of a call with your mother. Then the consequence: she thinks *you* hung up on her — and the phone, dying, shrugs the fallout onto its owner.
- **The punch lands at the end.** "That is your problem now." The phone's last act is not a farewell but a small, perfect act of revenge.
- **It is fresh.** The board at writing time (1,040 entries) had one mother joke (a passcode threat) and zero mid-call, hung-up or "your problem now" lines; no charger goodbye, no will reading, no pun on 1 percent, no search history.
- **It is tight.** One scene, three short sentences, 94 characters, every word earning its place.

Runner-up: "Your wallpaper is a beach you have never visited. I showed it to you 40,000 times. Book the ticket. Goodbye." — warmer and just as fresh, but a gentler laugh; the mid-call line wins on the bigger laugh and the sharper final beat.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt   # the line, exactly 94 characters, no trailing newline
```

`MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` overrides the output directory (default `/output`). No network, no randomness, no dependencies — the same run always writes the same 94 bytes.
